By default a subscription link is built on the panel's address. With a separate domain (for example `sub.example.com`) the panel's address is in no subscription link, and when the panel moves it is enough to move the domain: clients keep their links.

## Turn it on

<Steps>

1. Set up a domain or subdomain and point its A record at the panel's server. It must differ from the panel's domain.

2. **Settings → Subscription → A domain of its own for subscriptions**, the **Domain for links** field. Save.

3. The panel orders a certificate for the domain itself, and subscription links start to be built on it.

</Steps>

Links handed out before keep working. This domain opens subscriptions only: the admin panel stays on the panel's address. The **Remove the domain** button puts links back on the panel's address.

## Certificate

The **Certificate for subscriptions** card in the same setting shows how things stand. The panel serves a certificate by the name the client asks for, so the subscription domain has a certificate of its own and the panel another.

- **Automatic.** Let's Encrypt, as for the panel's domain: the check goes through port 80, so it must be open, see [Domain and certificates](/mikan/en/start/domain/). The **Get now** button orders it at once. If the order fails, the card shows the reason and a hint, and the button becomes **Retry**.
- **Your own.** The **Own certificate** button: paste a certificate and key for the domain, from certbot for example (`fullchain.pem` and `privkey.pem`). The panel serves it only to those who come to the subscription domain. Your own certificate wins over the automatic one. The **Back to the automatic one** button brings back the order from Let's Encrypt; while it is issued, clients get your certificate.

While the domain has no certificate, clients get the panel's, and apps may complain about the name. The subscription port can be moved out separately, see [Subscription port](/mikan/en/start/domain/#subscription-port).

## If it does not work

- The domain's record does not point at this server: Let's Encrypt cannot check the domain.
- Port 80 is held by another program: the certificate card names it, and the ways out are the same as for the panel.
- The domain is the panel's own or is an IP address: the panel refuses it, a different domain name is needed.
