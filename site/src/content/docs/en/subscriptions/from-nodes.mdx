Usually the panel serves the subscription link: the client comes to the panel's address and the panel renders the profile. When the panel's server is blocked or down, subscription updates break for clients — and renewals with them.

With this setting the nodes serve the profiles. The panel renders each profile once and sends the finished files to the chosen nodes; a node keeps them and answers on the same subscription address. The panel is only needed to refresh the data and change settings — clients no longer talk to it.

## What you need

- **A subscription domain** ([A domain for subscriptions](/subscriptions/domain/)): the link stays on it, only who answers it changes.
- **A node** of 0.5.2.x or newer, with the SNI router on 443 or its own page on 443 ([Sharing 443](/nodes/shared-443/)). If 443 belongs to one protocol with no router, the node cannot answer — the card says so.
- **DNS**: the subscription domain's A record must point at the chosen nodes (not at the panel). Mind the certificate limitation below.

## Turn it on

<Steps>

1. **Settings → Subscription → Serve subscriptions from nodes**: switch it on.

2. Tick the nodes that will answer. There may be several — then let DNS spread the subscription domain over them (several A records or round-robin); each gets the same set of profiles.

3. Point the subscription domain at those nodes. The panel shows, for each node, when it last sent it data and whether it answers the links.

</Steps>

Within a minute the nodes answer the links already handed out. The links themselves do not change: same domain, same path.

## How it works

- The panel sends the node finished profile bodies in every format (share links, Clash, sing-box, the page a browser gets) and the headers with traffic and term. The node renders nothing and looks nobody up: a path it was never given gets 404.
- A full reconcile runs every 15 minutes, and changes (a block, a tariff or term change, a deletion) arrive within a minute. Whatever is not in the new reconcile, the node deletes.
- A record lives until the subscription's end, or 30 days without a term: a panel gone for good does not hand out working profiles for ever. An expired record answers `410 Gone`, so the app says the subscription has ended instead of failing quietly.
- While the panel is unreachable the nodes keep serving the last data: the subscription keeps updating, and it can be renewed through the bot or the subscription page once the panel is back.

## Security

- The subscription path is an access token. The nodes and the panel keep it out of logs: only counters and hashes.
- The profile files live on the node in `subs/` of its data directory, mode 0600: only the node's process reads them.
- A node serves exactly what it was given and nothing about other subscribers: the node's public page and the monitoring page say not a word about subscriptions.

## Limitations

- **The subscription domain's certificate.** The panel orders and renews it on its own port 80. When the A record points at the nodes, Let's Encrypt reaches a node while the token stays on the panel: the certificate works until its first renewal (up to 90 days). Until the nodes order it themselves, keep the domain on the server that also answers the challenge — the panel or its own node — or leave “Serve subscriptions from nodes” off. In the plain setup, the panel and its node in one country, the panel serves the link itself and this setting is not needed.
- **Device binding**: the nodes get the subscriber's own keys (no separate key per device). The device limit still holds by addresses, but blocking or unbinding one device needs the panel reachable.
- **Setting changes** (protocols, routing, tariffs) go through the panel: the nodes get the new profiles at the next reconcile.
- A node without the SNI router and without its own page on 443 cannot answer — turn the router on ([Sharing 443](/nodes/shared-443/)).
